Component Characterization: 5-Pin Flick Switch

The TIMS control panel needed an octave switch, and the one I had was salvaged from a scrapped DVD/VCR player with no markings and no datasheet. Before designing around it, I measured which pins connect in each position, what the closed contacts add, and whether the open paths are isolated, against pass criteria set before testing.

Part
5-pin miniature flick switch, momentary, center-off. No part number or markings.
Source
Salvaged from the board of a scrapped DVD/VCR player (make and model unknown)
Use
TIMS Mk.1 control panel: octave down (left) and octave up (right) by default; reassignable in firmware
Test dates
Aug 30 and Sep 22, 2026 (initial checks); Oct 28, 2026 (full measurement)
Author
Anthony Rizvanov

Result: PASS, 6 of 6 criteria. Approved for the TIMS Mk.1 control panel.

Summary

The switch is a single-pole double-throw switch, momentary on both sides, with an off position at center, often written (ON)-OFF-(ON). Pins 2, 3 and 4 are one internal common. Pushing left connects pin 1 to it, pushing right connects pin 5, and pins 1 and 5 never connect to each other. The pin map was the same in all three test sessions, every closed path read within one meter count of the test-lead resistance, every open path read OL on the 2 MΩ range, and nothing changed after 100 actuations to each side.

Method

  • Equipment. Kobalt DT-926 digital multimeter: continuity, 200 Ω (0.1 Ω resolution) and 2 MΩ ranges. Not calibrated; zero checked by shorting the probes, 0.2 Ω before and after the session.
  • Fixture. A soldered hookup-wire lead on each pin (4.5 cm on pins 1 and 5, 6.5 cm on pins 2 and 4, 9.5 cm on pin 3), insulated with heat-shrink, joints covered in UV-cured resin. Readings were taken at the lead ends, so they include the wire and joints.
  • Orientation. Actuator up, pins facing the viewer, numbered 1 to 5 left to right. Position A is pushed left, toward pin 1; Position B is pushed right; center is rest.
  • Tests. (1) Mechanical inspection. (2) Continuity map of all 10 pin pairs in all three positions. (3) Contact resistance, three readings per closed pair with re-actuation between readings, plus a wiggle test. (4) Isolation of open pairs on the 2 MΩ range. (5) Body isolation. (6) 100 actuations to each side, then repeat of Tests 2 and 3.

  • Pin map. Pins 2, 3 and 4 are joined in every position and form the common. Center: pins 1 and 5 connect to nothing. Position A: pin 1 joins the common. Position B: pin 5 joins the common.
  • Mechanical. Three positions; both sides are momentary and spring back to center, with no click or detent. Contacts change state only at full travel. Body 25 × 15 mm, 10 mm at its thickest; actuator 5 mm; plastic housing.
  • Contact resistance. All six closed throw pairs read 0.1 Ω on all three readings, against 0.2 Ω for the shorted leads. Rocking the actuator while held changed nothing.
  • Isolation. 1–5 in all three positions, 1–2 at center and B, and 2–5 at center and A all read OL on the 2 MΩ range.
  • Cycling. After 100 actuations to each side the actuator had returned to center every time, and the map and readings were unchanged.

Analysis

Every closed path read 0.1 or 0.2 Ω against a lead reading of 0.2 Ω, so the contacts add less than the meter’s 0.1 Ω resolution. Readings of 0.1 Ω, below the lead reading itself, show the results sit at the limit of what this meter can resolve; they are not exact values. Small toggle switches are generally specified in the milliohm range, which fits this result. Three of the five pins are one common, so any of them can serve as the common terminal.

Limitations: one sample; an uncalibrated meter whose accuracy specification wasn’t on hand; readings that include the soldered leads; and the metal pivot rivet and actuator tab weren’t checked against the pins.

Conclusion and Next Steps

The switch met all six criteria and is approved for the TIMS Mk.1 control panel.

  • Wiring. Pin 3 (or 2 or 4) to ground, pin 1 to the octave-down input, pin 5 to the octave-up input. Each throw goes to its own microcontroller input with a pull-up resistor, so a press reads low and center reads high on both.
  • Design files. The pin map goes into the Mk.1 BOM, with a matching five-pin schematic symbol and footprint.
  • Before production. Replace this salvaged, unmarked part with a catalog switch of the same type that has a datasheet, repeat this test on it, and check the rivet and actuator tab against each pin.
