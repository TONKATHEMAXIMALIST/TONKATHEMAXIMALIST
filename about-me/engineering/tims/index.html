Project · Jul 2024 – present

TIMS

Trumpet Instrument: MIDI Synthesizer

A MIDI wind controller and standalone synthesizer meant to play like a real trumpet: breath sets the dynamics, three valves transpose and the lips choose the partial. I play jazz trumpet professionally, so the target is an instrument I would perform on.

Status: 9 prototype stages built, 3 worked. Professional four-board version designed, not yet fabricated.

Professional Version (Mk.1)

Handheld professional model · designed · not fabricated

The professional version is built around a Teensy 4.1 with an SGTL5000 audio adaptor. One processor hosts MIDI, synthesizes the audio and reads every breath, lip and valve sensor. Outputs: 5-pin DIN MIDI, USB-MIDI, headphone and line out. Power comes in at 9 V over USB-C Power Delivery and steps down to 5 V and 3.3 V.

All four are 2-layer boards. Netlists and the BOM are complete; nothing has been fabricated. Three smaller models are planned for other players and price points, from a desktop version to a handheld intermediate and advanced model.

Design Decisions

  • ESP32-S3 to Teensy 4.1. Processing headroom, native MIDI and audio synthesis, and all the sensor math on one chip.
  • 9 V input. Power flows one way, from the wall down to 5 V and 3.3 V, and the original 9 V display needs no boost stage.
  • Four boards. They follow the shape of the instrument, cut cost, make repair simpler and keep the sensor signals away from other noise.
  • Characterize before wiring. A 5-pin switch for the control panel came without a datasheet, so I measured it first. Read the switch report.
